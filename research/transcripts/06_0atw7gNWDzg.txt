If you selected a template during signup, you'll see that pricing model here. For this example, I'll create three services from scratch for my cleaning company. First, I'll click the plus button to add a category. Categories act as separate pages in your booking flow. Organizing your services into categories helps customers browse more easily and keeps the experience simple. I'll name this category residential cleaning, mark it as mobile only, and assign it an icon. Next, I'll add my first service. I'll click the plus button again and select service. Then I can give it a name, write a short description, upload an image, and set a price. To track my profit accurately, I can also enter the cost of the service. I'll mark it as mobile only and set the time it typically takes to complete. This is important when I'm getting booked back to back. By setting realistic durations, Field will prevent double bookings and ensure I arrive on time to each appointment without relying on arrival windows. At this point, I can save my service, but there are more tools available to help both my team and my customers. Let's start with checklists and photos. I can add checkboxes and questions for my workers to complete while on the job. These appear in the field worker app as they move through each task. I can also require before and after photos by specifying how many should be uploaded at each stage. While I can configure global checklists and photo requirements in my settings, adding service specific steps ensures everything gets done properly for jobs that need a little extra attention. Below that, I can turn on recurring booking options. I can suggest booking frequencies, offer discounts, and let customers choose a schedule that works best for them. If I don't turn this on, I can still manually create a recurring booking out of any service, but the customer won't be able to through the online portal. Next is customer questions. Just like checklist and photos, I can create global questions in my settings. But if this specific service needs more information, I can add questions just for it. If the service doesn't need as much detail, I can turn off the global questions to streamline the booking process. Lastly, I have the booking short link. Once everything is set, I can create the remaining two services. Since they are similar, I'll duplicate the first one and adjust the necessary information. And we're done.
